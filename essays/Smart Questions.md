## Questionable Importance
Each person has their own views on asking questions, which boils down to one of two viewpoints. One is that some people do not mind asking questions because they see it as a way to expand their knowledge on things that seem unfamiliar to them. The other is when people feel afraid to ask a question because they think their question might be dumb to ask. To me, asking questions is an important part of learning. There will be times when we won’t have an answer to something, and this is where we can ask a question. However, I see the sentiment behind why some think that there are dumb questions, which most professors and teachers would say, “There’s no such thing as a bad question.” However, some would say, “If you ask a dumb question, then expect a stupid answer.” So, to understand what makes a good question, we will dive into Eric Steven Raymond’s take on asking questions through his essay titled “How to Ask Questions the Smart Way.” 

## Formulating Questions 101
In his essay, Eric Steven Raymond provides a guide on how one should go about asking questions in an online forum or website. He starts by saying there’s a time when you should ask a question in an online forum, and it’s when you’ve gone through all other sources for answers, such as forum archives, the WEB, asking a friend, and more. The next step comes in when all of these resources are exhausted. If an answer is not found, then this is when you can pose a question for yourself, but as Raymond mentions, there are a few factors in posing your question. Those factors are: where, when, and how you should pose your question. The where includes picking the right forum to ask your questions, which Raymond says a good place would be Stack Overflow and other Web Forums. Next is knowing when you should pose your question. Raymond says a good tip is to search if your question has already been asked in this online forum. If your question hasn’t been asked, then it’ll be safe to ask your question. However, at this point, it is not safe to ask your question just yet because you now have to figure out how you should formulate your question. Raymond provides many ways to create the best question. One of those ways is having a meaningful subject header, which boils down to explaining and being informative about what you’re trying to ask. Having an informative question also needs to be a question that is grammatically correct and sound, as well as being accessible for people to view and be able to provide an answer that is well suited to your question. Using this information from Raymond’s essay, we will dive into what to do and what not to do in formulating a question in an online forum like Stack Overflow.

This is an example where the poster doesn’t start off with a question as their subheading, but instead they end their post with a question. In their subheading, the poster puts a problem they are having with their Datarow.RejectChanges function. In the beginning of their description, the poster provides details on what IDE they are working in and what it is trying to do. The poster also mentions that they did a few tests with their function and checked to see what was changed. They also checked for errors. Throughout the rest of the post, the poster provides a step-by-step explanation of what they were trying to do and also provides some code along with each step (which providing code within your question was something Eric Steven Raymond mentions in his essay). Once the poster finishes explaining their process, they end with a question asking, “What step am I missing?” Providing a step-by-step explanation within their post makes it easier for the poster to get readers to understand what the poster is trying to do. This allows readers to also work out what the poster was doing and formulate a solution to help the poster fix their problem.

Here is an excellent way to not ask a question. The poster is straight to the point in asking their question, but it comes with a caveat. The tone in their voice within their description sounds a bit demanding. Starting with “I need to know,” or “Tell me how” isn’t the best way to ask a question in an online forum. Additionally, the poster repeats three times that they want to use PySimpleGUI6 to make an app. From a reader’s perspective, it’ll be a bit annoying to read something they already know the poster wants when the poster has already written in their subheading what they are trying to accomplish. Also, ending with “I can make all sorts of things with it”, with it being their app in this case, poses a lot of questions about what those things can be. 



## Summary
To conclude, questions should be used as a way to communicate to others what we need help with. However, knowing how to formulate questions the right way can be beneficial not only to the person answering the question but also to the person asking the question. In doing so, we can turn dumb questions and stupid answers into smart questions and sharp answers.
